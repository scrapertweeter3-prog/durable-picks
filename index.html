Resale price is a durability number hiding in the listing

A filing cabinet that sells for $60 new and still pulls $35 used after twenty years of service is a $25 cabinet, no matter what the sticker says. The buy-it-for-life reviews on DurablePicks treat the used market as a durability instrument: if strangers will still pay real money for the thing once it is old, the build quality is not marketing copy.

The arithmetic is simple enough to run in the store. Subtract the realistic resale price from the new price, divide by the years of service you can document from listings of the same model, and you get an annual cost of ownership that no warranty card can fudge. A $240 pot that resells at $120 after two decades costs six dollars a year. A $130 nonstick pan replaced every decade and resold for nothing costs thirteen.

The pattern repeats across the categories on the site. Cast iron appreciates. Well-made tools hold half their price. Fast fashion resells for approximately zero, which is the market grading the seams. When a manufacturer's claim and the used market disagree, the used market has better data.

James Verlander, Poket Dev LLC, Detroit
